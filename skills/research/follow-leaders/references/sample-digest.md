# Fintech Leaders Digest — Jun 30–Jul 6, 2026

**The one thing worth knowing:** Two curated voices publicly disagreed this week
on whether embedded lending margins have permanently compressed or are cyclically
low — worth reading both sides before assuming either is settled.

## Podcasts

**"Why Banking-as-a-Service Consolidation Was Inevitable" — This Week in Fintech,
guest Jason Mikula**
- Argues BaaS platform failures over the past two years were a structural
  overcapacity problem, not a regulatory one — too many sponsor banks chasing too
  few compliant programs.
- Says the next wave of consolidation will happen at the program-management layer,
  not the bank layer.
- Pushes back on the popular narrative that stricter OCC guidance alone killed the
  sector.
- [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example1)

## LinkedIn

**Jason Mikula** published a long-form post on sponsor-bank due diligence checklists
BaaS programs should expect in 2026, with specific examples from recent program
reviews (not generic compliance advice). [Post](https://linkedin.com/example)

**Simon Taylor** argued in a newsletter-style LinkedIn post that "embedded finance"
is splitting into two markets: infrastructure plays with thin margins and vertical
SaaS bundles with pricing power — most startups are pitching the wrong one. [Post](https://linkedin.com/example2)

## X/Twitter

**Patrick Collison (@patrickc) and Alex Rampell (@arampell) disagree on lending
margins:** Collison shared usage data suggesting embedded lending take-rates are
stabilizing industry-wide; Rampell replied arguing the stabilization is temporary
and will compress further once more balance-sheet-light entrants launch this
year. [Thread](https://x.com/example)

**Ben Milne (@BenMilne)** shared a firsthand account of why Dwolla moved a core
payments flow off a third-party rail in-house, citing latency rather than cost as
the deciding factor. [Post](https://x.com/example2)
