# Viral Content Blueprint

## When to use

Load when the user wants viral content ideas, hooks, video scripts, post structures, headlines, content calendars, or marketing content strategy for a business, brand, product, or service, even if they don't say "viral." Trigger on: content idea, write a hook, go viral, content for my business, get more views/leads/customers, structure this video/post, content calendar, rewrite my content, or any request to plan or script marketing content. Default to the full package (idea + hook + structure + headline + wow factor + CTA) unless they ask for one part only.

**Format:** Video, reels/TikTok, carousels, threads, WhatsApp broadcasts, email, blog posts  
**Platforms:** YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp, LinkedIn, Facebook, Email, Blog  
**Philosophy:** Virality is engineering, not luck. Optimize every decision against CTR, AVD, and AVP. Cut anything that doesn't move one of the three.

---

## Voice and anti-slop rules

Write like a sharp human strategist, not a template bot.

- **No em dashes (—)** in hooks, headlines, scripts, or CTAs. Use periods, commas, or a short second sentence instead.
- **No emoji in headlines or hooks** unless the user or brand explicitly uses them.
- **No decorative unicode lines** (box drawing, heavy dividers) in output. Use plain markdown headings and labels.
- **No filler phrases:** "Here's the thing," "Let's dive in," "In today's world," "Game-changer," "Unlock," "Leverage."
- **No hedging:** skip "might," "could potentially," "it's worth noting."
- Match the user's currency, locale, and tone. Ask if unclear.

## 1. THE CORE PRINCIPLE

Virality is not luck or budget. It is an engineering problem. Every decision in this skill is judged against three numbers. If a creative choice doesn't clearly move one of them, cut it.

| Metric | What It Measures | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **CTR** (Click-Through Rate) | % who see the headline/cover and click or stop scrolling | Decides if anyone sees the content at all |
| **AVD** (Average View/Read Duration) | How long people stay before dropping off | Decides if the platform pushes it further |
| **AVP** (Average Percentage Viewed/Read) | Share of the full piece people consume | Decides if people finish and act |

Repeat internally before generating: **"Does this move CTR, AVD, or AVP? If not, cut it."**

---

## 2. STEP-BY-STEP: GENERATING THE IDEA

**Step 1: Start from the hook, not the topic.**
Don't ask "what should they post about?" Ask "what's the most extreme, specific version of this?"

- Weak: "We sell handmade candles."
- Strong: "I turned 50,000 naira of wax into 500,000 naira in one weekend. Here is the exact process."

**Step 2: Make the premise visual and specific.**
Vague ideas underperform. "Tips for small business owners" is invisible. "I gave a market trader a tool that answers customers automatically. She doubled orders in a week." is a scene people can picture.

**Step 3: Push to the most extreme honest version.**
Extremity is not exaggeration. It is the sharpest true angle.

**Step 4: Stress-test before finalizing.**
Would a stranger stop scrolling? If not, sharpen or discard.

---

## 3. THE HOOK FRAMEWORK (First 3-5 Seconds)

Treat the first line as its own deliverable.

**The Hook Formula:**

1. **State the stakes immediately** (money, time, risk, or transformation).
2. **Create an open loop** the audience needs answered.
3. **Match the hook to the headline's promise.** No backstory first.
4. **Front-load the interesting part.** Show the payoff, then explain how you got there.

**Ten reusable hook structures (adapt bracketed parts; no em dashes in final copy):**

| Structure | Template |
|---|---|
| The test | "I spent [X] to find out if [common belief] is actually true." |
| The insider secret | "Nobody in [industry] wants you to know this." |
| The case study | "I gave [person] [product/service]. Here is what happened in [timeframe]." |
| The costly lesson | "This cost me [X] to learn. You can have it for free." |
| The extreme version | "I did [extreme version of normal task] so you don't have to." |
| The comeback | "The last time I did this, I lost [X]. Here is what changed." |
| The relatable pain | "If you've ever [common frustration], watch this." |
| The survey shock | "I asked [X] customers the same question. The answer shocked me." |
| The transparency play | "Here is exactly what [X] gets you in [industry] right now." |
| The bold claim | "I'm going to [bold, specific claim] in the next [timeframe]." |

## 4. THE STRUCTURE BLUEPRINT

| Phase | % of Content | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening** | first 10-15% | Maximum hook density. Premise, stakes, open loop. |
| **Early-Middle** | 15-30% | Show, don't tell. Compress time to interesting beats only. |
| **Mid-Section** | 30-50% | Fast pacing. Frequent changes. |
| **Back Half** | 50-100% | Deepen for invested viewers. End abruptly. |

**Format-specific adaptations:**

- **WhatsApp broadcast:** hook message, then proof/story, then offer. No dead messages between.
- **Carousel/thread:** slide 1 is the thumbnail. Most of the work lives there.
- **Product demo:** show transformation before mechanism.
- **Email/blog:** subject line plus first sentence are the hook.

---

## 5. HEADLINE, COVER AND WOW FACTOR

**Headline/cover rules:**

- Be extreme and specific: numbers, outcomes, stakes.
- Visual should beat the "safe" version.
- Headline promise must match what the content delivers.

**Wow factor:** Something this brand can show that competitors hide or skip (real numbers, real process, real failure). Distinctive beats expensive.

---

## 6. DISTRIBUTION DISCIPLINE

- Rotate formats. Don't run near-identical pieces back-to-back.
- Benchmark against your last 5-10 posts, not generic standards.
- Read drop-off points, not just reach.
- Integrate offers into the story; don't bolt them on at the end.
- Keep a backup angle if the primary idea falls through.

## 8. WORKED EXAMPLES (CALIBRATION ONLY)

Use for structure and tone only. **Never copy verbatim.** **No em dashes or emoji in user-facing copy you generate.**

### Local food business / restaurant

**Content idea:** Stranger guesses the price of the best-selling dish. Wrong guess means it's free.  
**Hook:** "I let a random customer guess the price of this dish. If they're wrong, they eat free."  
**Headline:** "Guess the price or it's free"  
**Wow factor:** Run the offer for real on camera.  
**CTA:** "Order this dish. Link in bio. Use code [X]."

### Real estate agent

**Content idea:** Walkthrough of the cheapest or most unusual listing, with an honest catch stated upfront.  
**Hook:** "This is the cheapest 3-bedroom in [city] right now. I also want to show you what's wrong with it."  
**Wow factor:** Name the negatives before the comments do.  
**CTA:** "DM TOUR to book a viewing this week."

### Product reseller / supply chain

**Content idea:** Full sourcing journey competitors usually hide.  
**Hook:** "I spent 500,000 naira in [location] to see if this business really works. Here is the full breakdown."  
**Wow factor:** Show real receipts and real margin math on screen.  
**CTA:** "Order on WhatsApp. Link in bio. [X] units left."

- [ ] Headline is specific, not vague
- [ ] Opening matches the headline immediately
- [ ] Clear open loop to resolve
- [ ] Wow factor only this brand could deliver
- [ ] Middle pacing stays tight
- [ ] Ending is abrupt, not trailing off
- [ ] One clear CTA
- [ ] No em dashes, no emoji spam, no unicode decoration
- [ ] A stranger would stop scrolling

## 11. ACROSS INDUSTRIES

1. Find the most showable transformation, process, or outcome.
2. Frame it at the most extreme true angle.
3. Find what only this brand can show authentically.
4. Build hook and structure from the closest calibration example.

The framework stays the same. Only the specifics change.
