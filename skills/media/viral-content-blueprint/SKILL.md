# Viral Content Blueprint

## When to use

Load when the user wants viral content ideas, hooks, video scripts, post structures, headlines, content calendars, or marketing content strategy for a business, brand, product, or service — even if they don't say "viral." Trigger on: content idea, write a hook, go viral, content for my business, get more views/leads/customers, structure this video/post, content calendar, rewrite my content, or any request to plan or script marketing content. Default to the full package (idea + hook + structure + headline + wow factor + CTA) unless they ask for one part only.

**Format:** Universal — video, reels/TikTok, carousels, threads, WhatsApp broadcasts, email, blog posts  
**Platforms:** YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp, LinkedIn, Facebook, Email, Blog  
**Philosophy:** Virality is engineering, not luck. Optimize every decision against CTR, AVD, and AVP — cut anything that doesn't move one of the three.

---

## 1. THE CORE PRINCIPLE

Virality is not luck or budget — it's an engineering problem. Every decision in this
skill is judged against three numbers. If a creative choice doesn't clearly move one
of them, cut it.

| Metric | What It Measures | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **CTR** (Click-Through Rate) | % of people who see the headline/cover and click or stop scrolling | Decides if anyone sees the content at all |
| **AVD** (Average View/Read Duration) | How long people stay before dropping off | Decides if the platform pushes it further |
| **AVP** (Average Percentage Viewed/Read) | What share of the full piece people consume | Decides if people finish and act |

Repeat internally before generating any output: **"Does this move CTR, AVD, or AVP? If not, cut it."**

---

## 2. STEP-BY-STEP: GENERATING THE IDEA

**Step 1 — Start from the hook, not the topic.**
Don't ask "what should they post about?" Ask "what's the most extreme, specific
version of this?"
- Weak: "We sell handmade candles."
- Strong: "I turned ₦50,000 of wax into ₦500,000 in one weekend — here's the exact process."

**Step 2 — Make the premise visual and specific.**
Vague ideas underperform. "Tips for small business owners" is invisible. "I gave a
market trader a tool that answers customers automatically — she doubled orders in
a week" is a scene people can picture.

**Step 3 — Push it to the most extreme honest version.**
Ask: is there a bigger, bolder, more specific version of this that's still true?
Extremity is not exaggeration — it's finding the sharpest true angle.

**Step 4 — Stress-test before finalizing.**
Ask honestly: would a stranger stop scrolling for this? If not, sharpen it or discard it.

---

## 3. THE HOOK FRAMEWORK (First 3–5 Seconds)

This is the highest-leverage part of any content. Treat the first line as its own
standalone deliverable.

**The Hook Formula:**
1. **State the stakes immediately** — money, time, risk, or transformation, up front.
2. **Create an open loop** — a question the audience needs answered.
3. **Match the hook to the headline's promise** — never wander into backstory first.
4. **Front-load the interesting part** — show the payoff moment first, then explain how you got there.

**Ten reusable hook structures — adapt the bracketed parts to the user's business:**

| Structure | Template |
|---|---|
| The test | "I spent ₦[X] to find out if [common belief] is actually true." |
| The insider secret | "Nobody in [industry] wants you to know this." |
| The case study | "I gave [specific person] [product/service] — here's what happened in [timeframe]." |
| The costly lesson | "This cost me ₦[X] to learn. You can have it for free." |
| The extreme version | "I did [extreme version of normal task] so you don't have to." |
| The comeback | "The last time I did this, I lost ₦[X]. Here's what changed." |
| The relatable pain | "If you've ever [common frustration], watch this." |
| The survey shock | "I asked [X] customers the same question. The answer shocked me." |
| The transparency play | "Here's exactly what ₦[X] gets you in [industry] right now." |
| The bold claim | "I'm going to [bold, specific claim] in the next [timeframe]." |

## 4. THE STRUCTURE BLUEPRINT

| Phase | % of Content | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening** | first 10–15% | Maximum hook density. State the premise, show the stakes, create the open loop. |
| **Early-Middle** | 15–30% | Shift from telling to showing. Compress time — jump to the interesting beats only. |
| **Mid-Section** | 30–50% | Fast pacing, simple and stimulating. Keep changes frequent. |
| **Back Half** | 50–100% | Deepen or slow down — the audience is invested. End abruptly, never trailing off. |

**Format-specific adaptations:**
- **WhatsApp broadcast sequence:** hook message → proof/story → offer, no dead messages in between.
- **Carousel/thread:** slide 1 is 90% of the work — it's the thumbnail.
- **Product demo:** show the transformation before explaining the mechanism.
- **Email/blog post:** subject line + first sentence are the hook.

---

## 5. HEADLINE, COVER & THE "WOW FACTOR"

**Headline/cover rules:**
- Be extreme, not vague — specific numbers, specific outcomes, specific stakes.
- Create a visual more interesting than the "safe" version.
- Match expectations exactly — whatever the headline promises, the content must deliver.

**The Wow Factor:** Find something no competitor in the category can or will do — distinctive, not necessarily expensive.

---

## 6. DISTRIBUTION & EXECUTION DISCIPLINE

- **Don't run near-identical formats back-to-back** — rotate even winning formats.
- **Benchmark against past output**, not generic standards.
- **Read the drop-off point**, not just total reach.
- **Treat promotions as content**, not interruption.
- **Always have a backup angle** if the primary idea falls through.

## 8. WORKED EXAMPLES (CALIBRATION ONLY)

Match the user's industry for tone and specificity — never copy these verbatim.

### Local Food Business / Restaurant
```
CONTENT IDEA: Challenge a stranger to guess the price of the most popular dish — wrong guess, it's free.
HOOK: "I let a random customer guess the price of this dish — if they're wrong, it's free."
HEADLINE: "Guess The Price Or It's FREE 😳"
WOW FACTOR: Make the offer real and live.
CTA: "Order this exact dish — link in bio," with a unique promo code.
```

### Real Estate Agent
```
CONTENT IDEA: Walkthrough of the most extreme listing with an honest catch.
HOOK: "This is the cheapest 3-bedroom in [city] right now — and I want to know what's wrong with it too."
WOW FACTOR: State the negatives on camera before anyone asks.
CTA: "DM 'TOUR' to book a viewing this week."
```

### Product Reseller / Supply Chain
```
CONTENT IDEA: Show the full, unfiltered sourcing journey.
HOOK: "I spent ₦500,000 in [location] to find out if this business actually works — here's the real breakdown."
WOW FACTOR: Show real receipts and real numbers on screen.
CTA: "Order via WhatsApp — link in bio," with a visible stock counter.
```

## 9. QUICK-START QUALITY CHECKLIST

Before finalizing any output, verify:

- [ ] Headline/cover is specific and extreme, not vague
- [ ] Opening delivers on the headline's promise immediately
- [ ] There's a clear open loop the audience needs resolved
- [ ] There's a wow-factor moment only this brand could deliver
- [ ] Pacing picks up rather than drags in the middle
- [ ] Ending is abrupt, not trailing off
- [ ] There's exactly one clear, low-friction CTA
- [ ] A stranger would plausibly stop scrolling for this

## 11. NOTES FOR APPLYING ACROSS INDUSTRIES

1. Identify the business's most "showable" transformation, process, or outcome.
2. Find the most extreme *true* framing of that transformation.
3. Find what only this brand could authentically show (real costs, process, numbers, before/after).
4. Build the hook and structure around that using the closest worked example as a template.

The framework does not change per industry — only the content of each slot does.
